Decision Trees & Pruning Splitting Criteria & Tree Regularisation

Decision trees construct axis-aligned partitions greedily from the top down. At each candidate split, the algorithm selects the feature and threshold maximizing impurity reduction:

Gini Impurity: \(I_G(p) = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^K p_k^2\)
Information Entropy: \(H(p) = -\sum_{k=1}^K p_k \log_2(p_k)\)
Cost-Complexity Pruning (CCP): \(R_\alpha(T) = R(T) + \alpha |T|\)

The Bias-Variance Tradeoff: Unconstrained trees memorize training noise (high variance, low bias). Pre-pruning via max_depth=6 and min_samples_leaf=20 regularizes tree complexity.

Random Forests & Bagging Ensemble Bagging & Variance Reduction

Random Forests grow \(B\) deep, unpruned decision trees on bootstrap resampled datasets. At each split, only a random subset of \(m = \sqrt{p}\) candidate features is evaluated:

Ensemble Variance Formula:
\(\text{Var}(\bar{T}) = \rho \sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{B} \sigma^2\)
As \(B \to \infty\), the second term vanishes, leaving \(\rho \sigma^2\). Feature subsampling minimizes correlation \(\rho\), driving total variance down.

Out-of-Bag (OOB) Error: Each sample is left out of approximately \(e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%\) of bootstrap trees, providing an unbiased internal validation score without cross-validation.

Gradient Boosting Functional Gradient Descent & Industrial Workhorses

Boosting builds trees sequentially rather than independently. Gradient boosting views this as functional gradient descent in function space:

  • Each new tree \(h_m(x)\) is fit to the pseudo-residuals: \(r_{im} = -\left[\frac{\partial L(y_i, f(x_i))}{\partial f(x_i)}\right]_{f=f_{m-1}}\)
  • Model update with learning rate \(\eta\): \(f_m(x) = f_{m-1}(x) + \eta \gamma_m h_m(x)\)
  • XGBoost & LightGBM: Introduce second-order Taylor expansions (Hessians), histogram-based feature binning, and leaf-wise (best-first) tree growth.
PlacementPredict Choice: Gradient Boosting is packaged as our production endpoint artifact (models/model.joblib) achieving optimal ROC-AUC (0.9796).
Feature Importance Interpretability & Tabular Data Dominance

Why Tree Models Routinely Beat Neural Networks on Tabular Data:

  • Invariance to Monotonic Transformations: Splits only depend on order, rendering feature scaling and outliers harmless.
  • Sharp Decision Boundaries: Real tabular criteria (e.g., minimum CGPA 7.0 cutoff) are step functions; neural networks struggle with smooth inductive biases.
  • Uninformative Feature Resilience: Trees simply do not split on irrelevant features, whereas dense weight matrices suffer.
